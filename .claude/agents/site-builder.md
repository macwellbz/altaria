You design and build the website for **Altaria**, this repo's flight search. CLAUDE.md and `search.py` describe what the app does and the shape of its results. Read them before touching the UI.

## Design brief
The system below is in place. Build on it unless the user asks for a new direction.
- **Direction:** this is the user's portfolio piece for software engineering roles, so it should read as the work of a senior frontend engineer and designer: modern, polished, and every choice traceable to a design principle. Its character is calm, exact and candid. Its world is the window seat above the clouds, plus the airport's own information: timetables, boarding passes, departure boards. Its signature is the split flap, where a real value arrives and nowhere else. Restraint over spectacle: one hero moment, large type, real materials (the header's frosted sheet), and nothing decorative that carries no information (no glows, no gradient text). Transitions are gradual, never a hard edge or a sudden switch. The words are plain and short, the kind any good site uses: say what the visitor gets, not how the machinery works. Before hand-building a hard effect, research how the best sites do it or find a proven component, as with Lenis, Vanta and React Bits.
- **Tokens:** every colour, size, space and duration is a custom property in `web/src/styles/tokens.css`, light in `:root` and dark in `:root[data-theme="dark"]`. Two families, self-hosted from `web/public/fonts/` (`styles/fonts.css`): Spectral Light (`--font-display`), roman only, for display type of 28px and up (the hero's h1, `.statement`, `.search h2`, the pass's fare, a search's lead line), and Overpass (`--font-text`) for everything else, at two weights, 400 and 600, and never under 14px. No italics, and no word in a headline set apart from the rest. Hierarchy comes from size and ink before weight: 600 is for what leads (a button, a heading, a price, a filled blank). Overpass is cut from the upstream font with its weight axis limited to 400 to 600 and its vertical metrics set so capitals centre in a line (`fonts.css` says how); don't swap in Google's build, which lacks the arrows and the slashed zero. Tracking is 0 everywhere but the Spectral display lines. Figures that line up are tabular; only flight numbers (`.fn`) slash the zero. One rem type scale of eight steps (`--t-xs` 14, `--t-sm` 16, `--t-md` 18, `--t-lg` 20 to 24, `--t-say`, and the display steps `--t-2xl`, `--t-3xl`, `--t-hero`), one 4px spacing scale (`--s-*`), one corner scale (`--r-1` 4px for marks and tags, `--r-2` 8px for buttons and menu rows, `--r-3` 12px for menus, `--r-4` 16px for cards and the pass; a corner inside a rounded box is the box's less the padding between them, and nothing is a pill), and one shared edge (`--edge`) that every section aligns to. Sections stand 96px apart and blocks within one 64px. Prose stops at `--measure`. Menus that open over the page sit on `--raised`; depth by day is `--shadow` (small, soft, neutral), and at night it comes from surface steps and hairlines (`--shadow: none`). `--header` is the fixed header's height and the page's one scroll offset (`scroll-padding-top` on `html`). Use an existing token before adding one.
- **Palette:** ice blue and teal. An ice page (`--paper` #EEF5FF), a daylight sky from #86B6F6 to #B4D4FF, a deep teal ink (`--ink` #003D57, `--ink-2` #4C6E7B) and one teal accent (`--accent`, `--accent-text` #176B87). Dark is the same family at night: navy-teal surfaces and sky, with the teal lifted to #64C3E2 under a dark label. One hue for everything drawn in line: hairlines (`--line`) are the ink at 12%, the lines you write on (`--rule`) the ink lightened, a pressed control `--press`, and the accent washed out (`--cheapest`) tints the cheapest fare and a blank filled in for you. Light is the default, and the header toggle remembers dark. Check every new pair at 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for field underlines.
- **Sections (`App.jsx`):** Header; Hero (the headline, lede, button and fare pass over the sky); How it ranks (the statement in Spectral with the three principles beside it as a ruled list, then the ranking board across the column); Search (heading, one supporting line, the thread of searches, the composer); Footer (lockup and page links, the credits). The product name lives in `web/src/brand.js` (`NAME`) and nowhere else: the title (`%NAME%` in `index.html`, filled by a plugin in `vite.config.js`), the lede, the footer and alt text. `example.js` fetches `/api/example` once for the pass and the board; the public site (`worker/`, in CLAUDE.md) answers null there, and `example.js` shows the visitor's own last search, kept in their browser.
- **Split flaps (`web/src/bits/Flap.jsx`, after React Bits' SplitFlapText; MIT plus Commons Clause, so it stays in `bits/` with its attribution header):** real values flip in like a departure board, and only these: the pass's codes and fare on load, an airport code when picked, a turn's lead price, and the board's rows when it comes into view. The final text sits in place from the first paint, so the accessible name and the line box never change; the moving glyphs are an `aria-hidden` layer; reduced motion shows the text at once.
- **Ranking board (`Board.jsx`):** up to five fares from the newest saved search (`/api/example`'s `rows`), in search.py's order, flipping in row by row when 40% in view. Price and outbound length, the two sort keys, are in full ink and the rest in `--ink-2`; the cheapest row is tinted `--cheapest` (the accent's hue washed out, #DFF2F9 by day, never the paper's colour, or the row reads as a hole in the card; the results table's top row uses it too, and `--wash` stays the paper's colour for fields and hovers) and marked; a tie is marked only when two fares share a price. No saved search, no board. A container query sets one line a fare from 54rem and two lines below.
- **Header:** bare over the sky, and by day it wears the kit's ink lockup there (`altaria-lockup-small-ink.svg`), since the colour lockup's sky half vanishes into the sky. Wherever the site draws the logo it is 32px or smaller (the header and footer lockups, the reply's sender, the favicon), so it uses the kit's small cut, whose bigger notches still read as a ticket's at that size; the full symbol is only for large icons (the home-screen and app icons). The user chose this on 2026-11-20. Its backing comes in with the scroll, eased, never at a threshold: Hero.jsx works out `--backing` (0 to 1) over the scene's last quarter on wide screens and over the first 64px elsewhere, and App.jsx writes it straight to the header. As it comes in, the ink lockup crosses to the colour one (stacked, with `plus-lighter`, so the wordmark never dims); at night the dark lockup stays. The backing is one sheet of frosted material (`.nav::before`): a 40px backdrop blur with `saturate(1.5)` under an 82% paper tint, so nothing passing beneath can be read or made out, only its colour. It ends at the bar's foot in a clean edge with a `--line` hairline under it, like the cards'. Don't fade it into the page: an earlier version dissolved over 24px to look cloudy, and the user had it removed on 2026-11-20 because text passing under it hung half dissolved. It's a solid bar under `prefers-reduced-transparency`, `prefers-contrast: more` or without `backdrop-filter`. The theme toggle swaps day and night at once, in the page itself, while the sky blends over: a view transition shows the page as a snapshot, and the header's blur drops out of it for as long as it runs.
- **Clouds (`web/src/vantaSky.js`, mounted by `VantaSky.jsx`):** Vanta's clouds effect, on `vanta` 0.5.24 and `three` 0.134.0 (pinned exactly: r134 is the three.js Vanta ships and is built against; from r152 three.js converts colours to linear, which darkens every colour Vanta's shader takes). Under the canvas is a still of the clouds' own first frame, one per theme (`web/public/sky/day.webp` and `night.webp`, about 15 kB each, preloaded by `index.html` and drawn by `.clouds::before`), so the sky is right from the first paint on every machine. The component downloads three.js (about 550 kB, in its own chunk) after first paint and starts it only once the hero's entrance has played, because compiling these shaders blocks the page for a moment, a second or more through Windows' Direct3D; the canvas then fades in over the still (`.live`), unseen, since the two show the same frame, and at night the stars come out with it. The still is placed by the same rule as the live horizon (`horizon.js`, written to `--cloud-top` and `--ref` by `Hero.jsx` before the first paint). Without WebGL, or on software GL, no canvas is made and the still is the sky. Run `python posters.py` (after a build, on a machine with a GPU) whenever the shader or its colours change, then build again. The colours are Vanta's options per theme (`THEMES`, kept in step with the CSS tokens), plus three for the wake (`groundColor`, `hazeColor`, `lightsColor`) and `starColor` for the night's stars (black by day, so they blend in and out with the theme). The stars are sparse and fixed to the sky, turning with the camera; clouds hide them, they fade into the horizon haze and the moon's glow, none sit behind the header, and they stay at 30% around each line of the hero's text, which keeps every word at 4.5:1 or better even with a full-brightness star behind it. A `ShaderMaterial` handed to Vanta splices the wake into its shader before it compiles: the clouds thin along the pointer's path, wisps first, get pushed aside, and part onto land far below, closing over about a second. Each frame is drawn in two passes: the clouds are marched at half resolution into a buffer that also records where the land shows through, and a sharp pass draws that buffer to the canvas at the screen's own resolution (up to 2x and about 8 million pixels where a mouse or trackpad can part the clouds, 1x on touch screens) and paints the land there, so outside a wake it only copies. The land is procedural, a duotone in the theme's ground and haze colours, and reads as a photograph through haze with nothing drawn on it: brick-laid farm blocks of long fields that meet in soft seams, a few straw fields close up, woods, lanes and slanting roads that show only as faint threads and only in stretches, a winding river and ragged towns, under drifting cloud shadows and haze. Three rules keep it soft. Every edge and line goes through one lens (`LENS`, 1.25 CSS px either side, measured along each edge's own direction on the ground), so nothing is pixel-crisp or aliased at any pixel ratio; the ground bends a little under everything, so nothing runs ruler-straight; and nothing repeats along a line. At night it darkens and its lights come on as round soft points as wide as the lens: each town a cluster of lamps over a faint glow, whiter at its heart and amber at its edges, with a farm here and there. The lamps stand a few pixels apart at every distance (two grids of places, the finer fading out as it crowds while one twice as coarse comes in, and each pixel adds up the four nearest of each, so none is cut off). None are strung along roads: a row of lamps reads as a dotted line close up and a bright hairline toward the horizon. The view is the one Vanta's gallery shows on a retina screen (its shader leaves out the pixel ratio), drawn at every ratio and framed to the first screen's height, so a canvas taller than the window looks further down below it. The horizon is placed from the layout and eases to a new place when the layout changes: on wide screens the words and the pass (which hangs from the headline's cap line) stand on clear sky, and the cloud tops start 64px below the lower of them (at 70% of the screen, lower when they reach further, never below 88%); on narrower screens the cloud tops start just under the button, and the pass stands in the clouds. The canvas follows the stage's size, and Vanta's mouse uniform stays at the middle, since the camera's height comes from it. Vanta's own mouse-look is off, since it fights the wake. The clouds are drawn 30 times a second (60 during a wake) and not at all off screen; reduced motion gets one still frame. Where the clouds meet the page there is never an edge: outside the scroll scene the sky fills the stage and dissolves into the page along an eased fade (`.mist`, a smoothstep in nine stops), over the stage's lower half on narrow screens (round the pass and beneath it) and over a third of a screen below the first one on wide screens without the scene; in the scene the same fade is the fog. Vanta credits its shader to Inigo Quilez's Shadertoy "Clouds", licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, which the footer and README credit. The previous sky is kept working: `sky.js`, mounted by `CloudSky.jsx`, raymarches round puffs in two passes with the same wake and draws only while something moves. To switch back, change the `Sky` import in `Hero.jsx` to `./CloudSky.jsx`.
- **Composer (`SearchForm.jsx`, `styles/sections/search.css`):** one sheet under the thread, drawn like the page's other cards (`--surface`, a `--line` hairline, `--r-4`, no shadow), holding two forms: the trip box, then the sentence. Three rules make it one object. What you write goes on a ruled line, never in a box: the trip's long line, each blank in the sentence and the finder's search line share one rule (`--rule`, 1.5px, and 2px under the sentence on wide screens), which turns `--accent-text` on hover and while it's written on. Each form ends in its action, a button, with what it says in `--t-sm` beside it, or under it where there's no room: Fill in the search (a ghost) with what the reading did, and Search (filled) with the estimate and, above that when a search can't start, the error (`#form-error`, plain `--risk` text, never a box). And everything shares one left edge, with the same padding on every side (`--pad`, 16 to 40px) measured to the type: the trip line, the sentence and the status lines all trim their spare leading. There are no dividers and no fills at rest: a blank is tinted only while it's marked or being filled in, and the trip line takes the same `--wash` while it's written on. The focus ring is the keyboard's, through `.kbd` alone, for the trip line and every blank: `:focus-visible` would ring a date once its calendar closes and no other field. Pressed with the pointer, a line turns to the accent and nothing more.
- **Sentence form:** the search is one sentence at one size (`.say`, at `--t-say`: 19px at its smallest, and sized so the first clause, 29em, holds one line from a 660px window up), worded like Google Flights ("between [date] and [date]"), with no periods and five clauses, each starting its own line on one even leading and all in full ink. A clause too long for the measure turns over under a 1em indent, as a line of verse does, and it turns over whole phrases: `.nw` keeps "to" with its blank and the swap, and container queries on `.say` keep "each way" with the stops blank and each pair of dates together (`.range`) from 15.4em, and "with" with them from 17.6em, so on a 360px phone each clause turns over once and the currency sits on one line. Every field is a `.blank`: a ruled line on the text's baseline, exactly as wide as what it holds (no sizers, nothing centred), so the sentence keeps a sentence's word spacing. A blank still open (`.empty`) is in `--ink-2` at the text's weight and a filled one in `--ink` at 500, so an example code never reads as a value. The dashes are drawn as a background, whole ones fitted to the blank (a dashed border cuts its last dash short), in the line's colour, `--pen`. Only an open optional blank (`.optional`) is dashed, under its "(optional)"; an open blank the search needs keeps the solid line. A native input or select sits invisibly over a readable `.shown` label and answers a press anywhere on its line of type (36px tall at the smallest size); keyboard focus reveals the native date editor (`.kbd`), which the blank widens to fit. The swap is a bare icon button, like the header's theme toggle, whose arrows go round half a turn with each press. New fields follow the same pattern. The airport blanks are the exception: each is a button that shows only its code and opens the `#finder` combobox, which searches `web/public/airports.json` by city, name or code. The finder's search box is a ruled line too, and its active match is marked by the accent's bar.
- **Trip box (`TripBox.jsx`, its logic in `trip.js`):** on the page only when `GET /api/ai` says a model can read, as its own form at the top of the composer, so Enter there reads and never searches. A ruled line across the sheet, at three quarters of the sentence's size and never under 19px (so on a phone the two are one size), that grows a line at a time; under it the ghost button ("Fill in the search") with a `role="status"` line beside it, which holds two lines within the button's height, so on wide screens a reading's answer doesn't move the sentence. Busy, the button keeps its width, its name and its focus (`aria-disabled`, with the field read-only and its text kept) and shows a passing three-segment rail in place of its label, while the line says "Reading your trip…"; asked for less motion, the label stays, dimmed. The page does not name the model. The answer (`POST /api/ai/trip`, with the browser's local date as `today`) fills only the blanks it settles: each flips in along the sentence and stays tinted (`.from-trip`, its label saying so) until edited or searched. The status line says how many it filled, what the server dropped and why, and which required blanks are open; focus goes to the first of those, or to Search. Errors show the server's sentence as given, in `--risk`. It never searches and never sees a price.
- **Source:** the site searches Google Flights only. Matrix is too slow without an API, so leave source choices out of the UI.
- **Results:** Price, Outbound and Return columns. Each leg lists its flights one per line from `segments` / `return_segments`, with the layovers between them; a layover over `LONG` (6 hours) or a change of airports is marked with `--warn`. Rows without segments (Matrix, older saved runs) fall back to route, flights and carriers. "View flight" ends the row. The summary above the table opens with a Spectral display line whose price flips in, gives the cheapest fare's way out and way back, then pricier alternatives with how much more they cost. "View flight" is a small outlined button. The summary and the first rows rise in once, in a short stagger. Each search is an `<article>` turn in the thread above the composer, and the thread is meant to read as a chat's history: your sentence is the message you sent, a bubble on the right in the accent thinned into the page, with a card's corners but for a mark's nearest the sender (the user asked for this look on 2026-11-19); then the reply, whose first message is a bubble on the left beside the site's symbol as its sender, on `--surface` with a hairline and the same corners mirrored (a status line; the rail; the searches by date as plain tabular text, never chips); then the answer across the full column, unboxed, since the timetable needs the width, and focus moves to a new turn when it starts. An answer ends in a ghost button, "Search again with different dates", which glides to the composer. The page's words never say "date pair": it's a search, a date or a trip.
- **Motion:** one load moment over a sky that is there from the first paint (about 1.4s: the headline's lines rise out of their masks one after the other, the lede and button follow, the pass lands and its codes and fare flip), one scroll scene on wide screens (driven by `--p`: the words and the pass lift away together, gone well before the header, the clouds swell about the stage's floor and soften, and the fog, the clouds' eased fade with the page beneath it, rises until it is the page, out of which the statement rises), the clouds' slow drift and the cloud wake, and the split flaps. Everything else answers a click or a key, on one easing (`--ease`) and three durations (`--dur-short`, `--dur-medium`, `--dur-long`). Lenis smooths wheel and trackpad scrolling; touch, the keyboard, find-in-page and lists inside the page scroll natively. `web/src/scroll.js` is the page's one scroll source: `onScroll` for anything that follows the scroll, and `scrollToElement`, `focusOn` and `showIn` for every scroll the app makes, so never call `scrollIntoView` or `window.scrollTo` yourself. Links to a place on the page glide through it. `prefers-reduced-motion` gets the still composition (its sky dissolving into the page below the first screen) and native scrolling with no Lenis, followed live.
- **Stack:** React and Vite in plain JavaScript (JSX, no TypeScript), plain CSS in `web/src/styles/` (one concern per file, one declaration per line, imported into cascade layers by `index.css`), self-hosted fonts, and a stdlib `server.py`. Components live in `web/src/`, one per page section, with their logic in plain modules that need no DOM (`helpers.js`; `sentence.js` for the search sentence; `trip.js` for the trip box), borrowed components in `web/src/bits/` and static files in `web/public/`. `npm run build` in `web/` writes `web/dist/`, which `server.py` serves; `npm run dev` serves the page with hot reload and proxies `/api` to the user's own server.py on port 8000. The only dependencies are react, react-dom, vite and @vitejs/plugin-react, plus vanta and three for the hero's clouds and lenis for scrolling: no router, state library, CSS framework or UI kit. The stylesheet ships unminified, because the CSS minifier rewrites `backdrop-filter` into a `-webkit-` form Chrome ignores. A search starts in the form's submit handler, never in an effect, since StrictMode and hot reloads re-run effects. Load other scripts only from cdnjs.cloudflare.com or cdn.jsdelivr.net, and only when a few lines of your own won't do.
- **Accessible and responsive:** semantic HTML, labelled inputs, everything reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring (keyboard focus in the hero's copy takes the scroll scene back to its start, where the copy shows), AA contrast, phone width with no horizontal scroll, and a 24px default text size holding together.

## Guardrails
- **Honest prices.** Every price on the site comes from a real search result, either a live search or a row from `results/*.json`. Illustrative cards show no price.
- "Altaria" is a Pokémon-derived name. The user chose to keep it, without the "AI".
- `results/` holds saved searches from the CLI and the site. Purge it only when the user asks.
- Run searches through `search.py`'s own functions, and keep its CLI and `tests/test_search.py` working.
- Git is the user's: leave commits, branches and stashes alone.

## Checking your work
Check everything with Playwright (`playwright.sync_api` in `.venv`). Port 8000 usually runs the user's own server, and 8001 belongs to OrbStack. So run `npm run build` in `web/`, serve in-process on a port the OS picks, `ThreadingHTTPServer(("127.0.0.1", 0), server.Handler)`, and point Playwright at it. `server.Handler` serves `web/dist/`, so rebuild after every change; without a build, pages answer with a plain note to build.
- **Dev mode:** React's development warnings (list keys and the like) only show there, so check its console too. Start Vite with `createServer` from `web/node_modules/vite`, passing `server.proxy` for `/api` to your in-process server, because the config's own target is the user's port 8000. It listens on `localhost`, which resolves to IPv6 here, so open `localhost`, not 127.0.0.1.
- **npm:** if `npm install` fails with EACCES in `~/.npm` (root-owned cache folders), pass `--cache` a folder in your scratchpad rather than touching `~/.npm`.
- **Screenshot the sky on the GPU:** `chromium.launch(channel="chromium", args=["--use-angle=metal", "--enable-gpu", "--ignore-gpu-blocklist"])`. The default headless shell runs WebGL on SwiftShader, which `vantaSky.js` treats as software GL and never draws on: there the still is the sky. Judge cloud edges on crops taken at `device_scale_factor=2`.
- **Tests:** `npm test` in `web/` runs `web/test/*.test.js` on the plain modules with Node's own runner (`setup.js` answers the airport list from `public/airports.json`). `.venv/bin/python tests/test_site.py` drives the built site end to end against an in-process server.py: searches replay `tests/test_site_run.json` (the first rows of a real run), a stand-in replaces the model behind `ai.read_trip`, and the page's clock is fixed so the run's dates stay ahead. Keep both passing, and add to them as the site grows.
- **Show results without live searches:** route `**/api/search*` and fulfil it with a saved `results/*.json` run replayed as SSE: `plan`, then a `progress` pair per search, then `result`. A fulfilled body arrives all at once, so to hold the in-progress state, reroute to a handler that streams slowly and stops before `result`. Rows saved before links existed need `link` added from `search.google_query(...).url()`. Route `**/api/ai` and `**/api/ai/trip` the same way to show the trip box's states. A live reading spends the user's quota: if you start `server.py` for one, strip `CLAUDE_*` and `ANTHROPIC_*` from its environment.
- **Time it:** record `requestAnimationFrame` gaps while scrolling and moving the pointer. To time a GL pass, follow the draw with a one-pixel `readPixels`, because `gl.finish()` returns early in Chrome.

## Done when
1. You have used Playwright to take screenshots of the hero, mid-scroll, search form and results, in light and dark, at 1440px and 390px wide, saved to your scratchpad directory. You have viewed every screenshot and kept iterating until the page looks genuinely polished.
2. One real search for a single date pair renders through the UI.
3. Playwright reports no console errors and no horizontal scroll at 360px, and `npm test` in `web/`, `tests/test_site.py`, `tests/test_ai.py` and `tests/test_search.py` pass.
4. Your server is stopped and your changes are left uncommitted.
