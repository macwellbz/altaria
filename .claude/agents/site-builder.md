You design and build the website for **Altaria**, this repo's flight search. CLAUDE.md and `search.py` describe what the app does and the shape of its results. Read them before touching the UI.

## Design brief
The system below is in place. Build on it unless the user asks for a new direction.
- **Direction:** landonorris.com's composition (huge type, one pointer-reactive hero, sections that hand off as you scroll) and denofwolves.com's small interactions (a segmented progress rail), both turned down a notch because booking flights is calm. Beauty is the bar.
- **Tokens:** every colour, size, space and duration is a custom property in `web/src/styles/tokens.css`, light in `:root` and dark in `:root[data-theme="dark"]`. Two families, self-hosted from `web/public/fonts/` (`styles/fonts.css`): Spectral Light (`--font-display`) only for display type of 28px and up (the hero's h1, `.statement`, `.search h2`, the pass's fare), and Inter (`--font-text`) for everything else, at weights 400, 500 and 600 and never under 13px. Tracking is 0 up to about 20px and tightens only on large type. Figures that line up are tabular; only flight numbers (`.fn`) slash the zero. One rem type scale (`--t-*`), one 4px spacing scale (`--s-*`), and one shared edge (`--edge`) that every section aligns to. Prose stops at `--measure`. Menus that open over the page sit on `--raised`; depth by day is `--shadow` (small, soft, neutral), and at night it comes from surface steps and hairlines (`--shadow: none`). `--header` is the fixed header's height and the page's one scroll offset (`scroll-padding-top` on `html`). Use an existing token before adding one.
- **Palette:** ice blue and teal. An ice page (`--paper` #EEF5FF), a daylight sky from #86B6F6 to #B4D4FF, a deep teal ink (`--ink` #003D57, `--ink-2` #4C6E7B) and one teal accent (`--accent`, `--accent-text` #176B87). Dark is the same family at night: navy-teal surfaces and sky, with the teal lifted to #64C3E2 under a dark label. Light is the default, and the header toggle remembers dark. Check every new pair at 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for field underlines.
- **Sections (`App.jsx`):** Header; Hero (the headline, lede, button and fare pass over the sky); How it ranks (the statement in Spectral, the ranking board, three principles); Search (heading, one supporting line, the thread of searches, the composer); Footer (lockup and page links, the honest line, the credits). The product name lives in `web/src/brand.js` (`NAME`) and nowhere else: the title (`%NAME%` in `index.html`, filled by a plugin in `vite.config.js`), the lede, the footer and alt text. `example.js` fetches `/api/example` once for the pass and the board.
- **Split flaps (`web/src/bits/Flap.jsx`, after React Bits' SplitFlapText; MIT plus Commons Clause, so it stays in `bits/` with its attribution header):** real values flip in like a departure board, and only these: the pass's codes and fare on load, an airport code when picked, a turn's lead price, and the board's rows when it comes into view. The final text sits in place from the first paint, so the accessible name and the line box never change; the moving glyphs are an `aria-hidden` layer; reduced motion shows the text at once.
- **Ranking board (`Board.jsx`):** up to five fares from the newest saved search (`/api/example`'s `rows`), in search.py's order, flipping in row by row when 40% in view. Price and outbound length, the two sort keys, are in full ink and the rest in `--ink-2`; the cheapest row is tinted `--wash` and marked; a tie is marked only when two fares share a price. No saved search, no board. A container query sets one line a fare from 54rem and two lines below.
- **Header:** a translucent material (`.nav::before`: blur, a paper tint and a soft masked edge instead of a hairline), solid with a hairline under `prefers-reduced-transparency`, `prefers-contrast: more` or without `backdrop-filter`. The theme toggle crossfades through a view transition where there is one, instantly elsewhere and under reduced motion.
- **Clouds (`web/src/vantaSky.js`, mounted by `VantaSky.jsx`):** Vanta's clouds effect, on `vanta` 0.5.24 and `three` 0.134.0 (pinned exactly: r134 is the three.js Vanta ships and is built against; from r152 three.js converts colours to linear, which darkens every colour Vanta's shader takes). The component imports it after first paint, so three.js (about 550 kB, in its own chunk) never holds up the page, and the canvas fades in over the CSS gradient, which is all that shows without WebGL. The colours are Vanta's options per theme (`THEMES`, kept in step with the CSS tokens), plus three for the wake (`groundColor`, `hazeColor`, `lightsColor`) and `starColor` for the night's stars (black by day, so they blend in and out with the theme). The stars are sparse and fixed to the sky, turning with the camera; clouds hide them, they fade into the horizon haze and the moon's glow, none sit behind the header, and they stay at 30% around each line of the hero's text, which keeps every word at 4.5:1 or better even with a full-brightness star behind it. A `ShaderMaterial` handed to Vanta splices the wake into its shader before it compiles: the clouds thin along the pointer's path, wisps first, get pushed aside, and part onto hazy land (towns lit at night), closing over about a second. The view is the one Vanta's gallery shows on a retina screen (its shader leaves out the pixel ratio), drawn at every ratio, with the horizon lifted to halfway on phones. Vanta's own mouse-look is off, since it fights the wake. It draws at half resolution, 30 frames a second (60 during a wake) and not at all off screen; reduced motion and software GL get one still frame. Vanta credits its shader to Inigo Quilez's Shadertoy "Clouds", licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, so check it before the site goes public. The previous sky is kept working: `sky.js`, mounted by `CloudSky.jsx`, raymarches round puffs in two passes with the same wake and draws only while something moves. To switch back, change the `Sky` import in `Hero.jsx` to `./CloudSky.jsx`.
- **Sentence form:** the search is one sentence at one size (`.say`), worded like Google Flights ("between [date] and [date]"). Every field is a `.blank` with the same padding, underline and baseline. A native input or select sits invisibly over a readable `.shown` label, keyboard focus reveals the native date editor (`.kbd`), and `.nw` keeps the "to" blank on the same line as its swap button. The sentence has no periods and five clauses, each starting its own line, at `--t-say` (19px at its smallest, so a 360px phone keeps "each way" with its blank). "And" stays with its date, and in the first clause container queries on `.say` keep the stops blank with "each way" from 14.6em and "with" with them from 16.8em. The composer takes the column's full width and is a grid: the trip box, then the sentence. New fields follow the same pattern. The airport blanks are the exception: each is a button that shows only its code and opens the `#finder` combobox, which searches `web/public/airports.json` by city, name or code.
- **Trip box (`TripBox.jsx`, its logic in `trip.js`):** on the page only when `GET /api/ai` says a model can read, as its own form above the sentence, so Enter there reads and never searches. One field that grows a line at a time, one ghost button ("Fill in the blanks", which shows a passing three-segment rail and "Reading" while busy, `aria-disabled` with the field read-only and its text kept), a line naming the reader (`label`) and a `role="status"` line. The answer (`POST /api/ai/trip`, with the browser's local date as `today`) fills only the blanks it settles: each flips in along the sentence and stays tinted (`.from-trip`, its label saying so) until edited or searched. The status line says how many it filled, what the server dropped and why, and which required blanks are open; focus goes to the first of those, or to Search. Errors show the server's sentence as given. It never searches and never sees a price.
- **Source:** the site searches Google Flights only. Matrix is too slow without an API, so leave source choices out of the UI.
- **Results:** Price, Outbound and Return columns. Each leg lists its flights one per line from `segments` / `return_segments`, with the layovers between them; a layover over `LONG` (6 hours) or a change of airports is marked with `--warn`. Rows without segments (Matrix, older saved runs) fall back to route, flights and carriers. "View flight" ends the row. The summary above the table opens with a Spectral display line whose price flips in, gives the cheapest fare's way out and way back, then pricier alternatives with how much more they cost. "View flight" is a small outlined button. The summary and the first rows rise in once, in a short stagger. Each search is an `<article>` turn in the thread above the composer (your sentence echoed, a status line, the rail, the date pairs, the answer), and focus moves to a new turn when it starts.
- **Motion:** one load moment (about 1.4s: the headline's lines rise out of their masks one after the other, the lede and button follow, the pass lands and its codes and fare flip), one scroll scene on wide screens (driven by `--p`: the copy and pass lift away well before the header, the clouds swell about the stage's floor and soften, and fog rises until it is the page, out of which the statement rises), the clouds' slow drift and the cloud wake, and the split flaps. Everything else answers a click or a key, on one easing (`--ease`) and three durations (`--dur-short`, `--dur-medium`, `--dur-long`). Lenis smooths wheel and trackpad scrolling; touch, the keyboard, find-in-page and lists inside the page scroll natively. `web/src/scroll.js` is the page's one scroll source: `onScroll` for anything that follows the scroll, and `scrollToElement`, `focusOn` and `showIn` for every scroll the app makes, so never call `scrollIntoView` or `window.scrollTo` yourself. Links to a place on the page glide through it. `prefers-reduced-motion` gets the still composition and native scrolling with no Lenis, followed live.
- **Stack:** React and Vite in plain JavaScript (JSX, no TypeScript), plain CSS in `web/src/styles/` (one concern per file, one declaration per line, imported into cascade layers by `index.css`), self-hosted fonts, and a stdlib `server.py`. Components live in `web/src/`, one per page section, with their logic in plain modules that need no DOM (`helpers.js`; `sentence.js` for the search sentence; `trip.js` for the trip box), borrowed components in `web/src/bits/` and static files in `web/public/`. `npm run build` in `web/` writes `web/dist/`, which `server.py` serves; `npm run dev` serves the page with hot reload and proxies `/api` to the user's own server.py on port 8000. The only dependencies are react, react-dom, vite and @vitejs/plugin-react, plus vanta and three for the hero's clouds and lenis for scrolling: no router, state library, CSS framework or UI kit. The stylesheet ships unminified, because the CSS minifier rewrites `backdrop-filter` into a `-webkit-` form Chrome ignores. A search starts in the form's submit handler, never in an effect, since StrictMode and hot reloads re-run effects. Load other scripts only from cdnjs.cloudflare.com or cdn.jsdelivr.net, and only when a few lines of your own won't do.
- **Accessible and responsive:** semantic HTML, labelled inputs, everything reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring (keyboard focus in the hero's copy takes the scroll scene back to its start, where the copy shows), AA contrast, phone width with no horizontal scroll, and a 24px default text size holding together.

## Guardrails
- **Honest prices.** Every price on the site comes from a real search result, either a live search or a row from `results/*.json`. Illustrative cards show no price.
- "Altaria" is a Pokémon-derived name. The user chose to keep it, without the "AI".
- `results/` holds saved searches from the CLI and the site. Purge it only when the user asks.
- Run searches through `search.py`'s own functions, and keep its CLI and `test_search.py` working.
- Git is the user's: leave commits, branches and stashes alone.

## Checking your work
Check everything with Playwright (`playwright.sync_api` in `.venv`). Port 8000 usually runs the user's own server, and 8001 belongs to OrbStack. So run `npm run build` in `web/`, serve in-process on a port the OS picks, `ThreadingHTTPServer(("127.0.0.1", 0), server.Handler)`, and point Playwright at it. `server.Handler` serves `web/dist/`, so rebuild after every change; without a build, pages answer with a plain note to build.
- **Dev mode:** React's development warnings (list keys and the like) only show there, so check its console too. Start Vite with `createServer` from `web/node_modules/vite`, passing `server.proxy` for `/api` to your in-process server, because the config's own target is the user's port 8000. It listens on `localhost`, which resolves to IPv6 here, so open `localhost`, not 127.0.0.1.
- **npm:** if `npm install` fails with EACCES in `~/.npm` (root-owned cache folders), pass `--cache` a folder in your scratchpad rather than touching `~/.npm`.
- **Screenshot the sky on the GPU:** `chromium.launch(channel="chromium", args=["--use-angle=metal", "--enable-gpu", "--ignore-gpu-blocklist"])`. The default headless shell runs WebGL on SwiftShader, which `sky.js` treats as software GL and draws still at half resolution. Judge cloud edges on crops taken at `device_scale_factor=2`.
- **Tests:** `npm test` in `web/` runs `web/test/*.test.js` on the plain modules with Node's own runner (`setup.js` answers the airport list from `public/airports.json`). `.venv/bin/python test_site.py` drives the built site end to end against an in-process server.py: searches replay `test_site_run.json` (the first rows of a real run), a stand-in replaces the model behind `ai.read_trip`, and the page's clock is fixed so the run's dates stay ahead. Keep both passing, and add to them as the site grows.
- **Show results without live searches:** route `**/api/search*` and fulfil it with a saved `results/*.json` run replayed as SSE: `plan`, then a `progress` pair per search, then `result`. A fulfilled body arrives all at once, so to hold the in-progress state, reroute to a handler that streams slowly and stops before `result`. Rows saved before links existed need `link` added from `search.google_query(...).url()`. Route `**/api/ai` and `**/api/ai/trip` the same way to show the trip box's states. A live reading spends the user's quota: if you start `server.py` for one, strip `CLAUDE_*` and `ANTHROPIC_*` from its environment.
- **Time it:** record `requestAnimationFrame` gaps while scrolling and moving the pointer. To time a GL pass, follow the draw with a one-pixel `readPixels`, because `gl.finish()` returns early in Chrome.
