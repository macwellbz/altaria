Altaria logo, round 6 · November 2026

Four logo directions and three names

One refresh of the Horizon Ticket, and three new marks taken from what the product actually does: it prices every date pair, sorts round trips by price and then flight time, and shows the answer the way a departure board would. Every mark is flat, uses three colours at most, reduces to one, and holds at 16 px. Pick a mark and a name together; every pairing is in the grid near the end.

Character
Calm, exact, candid.
World
The window seat above the clouds, and the airport's own information: timetables, boarding passes, departure boards.
Motion
The split-flap. Codes and fares flip into place, and each mark below shows how it would too.
Type
Spectral 300 for headlines, Inter for everything else. The wordmark is redrawn in Spectral and outlined.
Not again
Window, seal, summit, ridgelines, water, the landscape boarding pass and the i-dot sun were seen in rounds 1 to 5 and stay retired.

What it is

  • Flat sky. The gradient is gone: three flat colours and nothing else.
  • One module. Tear and ring are each 1/16 of the ticket, one pixel at 16 px. The tear stays on the golden section (sky : stub = 1.618).
  • Continuous corners, the curve app icons use, so the outline no longer kinks where straight meets round.
  • A true small cut. 16 and 32 px are redrawn on the pixel grid with larger notches, so the tab icon still reads as a ticket.

Why it could be the one

It keeps five rounds of decisions and the sun you already chose. Of the four, it is the only one that carries the window seat's feeling, sky over a horizon, rather than the airport's information.

Risk

It belongs to the old dawn story more than to boards and timetables, and it needs all three colours: in one colour the sun becomes a hole.

How it moves

The sky half flips down like a board flap, and the sun is on it.

What it is

  • Two halves on a hinge. The top flap is a shade lighter, as it is on a real board, which also keeps the hinge legible at 16 px.
  • A board zero. Condensed like split-flap figures, cut by the hinge. Coral is the character and the only warm thing in the mark.
  • Why not a round O. It was tried first. Split by a hinge in one colour, a circle reads as a Poké Ball, which a project leaving a Pokémon name can't risk.

Why it could be the one

It is the site's signature motion as a mark. In the header it can flip from blank to 0 on load with the same flip the fares use. It reads as a board character at 16 px, and the zero states the promise without a word.

Risk

A zero can read as “no results”. In one colour at 16 px it sits near a Greek theta, and its rounded outline is a cousin of the round-one porthole, though on a flap it reads as a digit.

Recommendation

Spectral 400, sentence case, tracked in by half a percent, outlined. It is the headline face, so the logo and the page speak with one voice, and a serif name next to Inter navigation reads as the brand rather than one more link. Sentence case writes the name the way you would say it, which suits candid better than lowercase's softness. 400 rather than the headlines' 300 because the logo sits at 20 to 30 px beside a solid symbol, where 300's hairlines go grey.

Why there is no “ai”

The site runs no model: the fares are real search results, sorted, and the summary is filled from templates. In a brand built on being candid, “ai” would be the one claim on the page that isn't true, and it dates the name. If free-text questions answered by a model ever ship, say so in that feature's copy, not in the logo.

Mark: Split Flap

It is the only mark that is the site's own motion: the logo can flip in on load exactly as the fares do, which makes the brand and the interface one idea, and that is the story a portfolio wants to tell. It reads at 16 px, works in one colour, and moves the brand from the old dawn imagery into the board-and-timetable world the redesign is built on. If you would rather keep continuity, the refined Horizon Ticket is the second choice and is ready to use as it is.

Name: Plainfare

It says what you get and how in two syllables, it came back clear in the search, and it works with all four marks. With the flap the name and mark don't repeat each other: the name says plain fares, the board says nothing sponsored. Fareboard is the more literal partner for the flap, and the runner-up, but its nearest neighbour, Fareboom, sells flights.

Before it goes public

Run a trademark search on the chosen name (USPTO, CIPO and EUIPO, classes 9, 39 and 42) and check the domain, then a reverse image search on the chosen mark. Spectral is under the SIL Open Font License, which allows its use in a logo. Once you pick, the final kit gets the icon set, the full small-size family and a usage guide, as round 5 did.

Round 6 concepts. Symbols are drawn on a 256-unit grid with separate 32 and 16 px cuts; files are in svg/ and png16/. Rounds 1 to 5 and the current kit are unchanged in brand/concepts/ and brand/final/.
